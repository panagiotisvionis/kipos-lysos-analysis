\opening{Dear Editors,}

We are pleased to submit our manuscript entitled \textbf{``Enhancing
Socio-Emotional Functioning of Adults with Disabilities: Evaluation of an
Experiential Intervention in a Day Care Facility''} for consideration as a
Full Article in \textit{Research in Developmental Disabilities}.

\textbf{What the study is about.}
The manuscript reports a three-phase longitudinal evaluation of a structured,
arts-based socio-emotional program delivered over three months in a Greek Day
Care Center for adults with intellectual and developmental disabilities
($n = 30$; 80\% with a documented intellectual disability, the remainder with
psychotic disorder, autism spectrum disorder, or cerebral palsy, with frequent
co-occurring conditions). The program, \textit{``The Universe Within Us,''}
combined theatre, music, movement, ceramics, and narrative dialogue in a small-group
format grounded in Social and Emotional Learning, emotional intelligence theory,
Vygotsky's socio-cultural framework, and Yalom's therapeutic group factors.

\textbf{Design and findings.}
Socio-emotional functioning was rated at baseline, mid-intervention, and
post-intervention by three independent sources: beneficiary self-report, two
trainers/educators, and an external observer. All three sources showed
statistically significant improvement between Phase A and Phase C
(paired $t$-tests, all $p < .001$; Cohen's $d$ = 2.45, 1.35, and 1.65
respectively), and Friedman tests confirmed progressive change across the three
phases. We interpret these results cautiously: the design has no control group,
the instrument was developed for this study, and the very large self-report
effect is discussed in relation to ceiling, social-desirability, and
response-shift explanations. The convergence of the external observer's ratings
with the other two sources is, in our view, the study's most informative
finding.

\textbf{Why \textit{Research in Developmental Disabilities}.}
The journal's scope---applied research across the range of developmental
disabilities, with attention to intervention, participation, and quality of
life---matches both the mixed-diagnosis population of community day services
and the applied, replicable character of the program. The study addresses a
population (adults in non-institutional day care) and a setting (Southern
European community services) that remain underrepresented in the intervention
literature, and it reports a documented intervention protocol, a pseudonymized
dataset, and reproducible analysis code.

\textbf{Declarations.}
The manuscript has not been published previously and is not under
consideration elsewhere. All four authors have approved the final version and
its submission. Three authors are affiliated with the Day Care Center in which
the study was conducted; this is disclosed in the manuscript and title page,
and there is no financial conflict of interest. The study was approved by the
facility's administration on 1~October~2025 and conducted in accordance with
the Declaration of Helsinki; informed consent was obtained from participants or
their legal guardians. The pseudonymized analysis dataset and all analysis code
are publicly available in a GitHub repository (URL on the title page). In line
with the journal's double-anonymized review policy, the main manuscript file
contains no identifying information; author details appear only on the
separate title page. Highlights are provided as a separate file.
